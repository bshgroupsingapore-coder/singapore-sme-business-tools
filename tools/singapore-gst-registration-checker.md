## What Is GST Registration?

Goods and Services Tax (GST) is a consumption tax imposed on goods and services supplied in Singapore and on certain imported goods and services.

Businesses that meet the applicable GST registration requirements may need to register with IRAS and account for GST on their taxable supplies.

Whether registration is required depends on the business's circumstances and the applicable GST rules.

## Compulsory vs Voluntary GST Registration

GST registration may be compulsory when a business meets the applicable registration requirements.

Businesses that do not have an obligation to register may also be able to consider voluntary GST registration, subject to the applicable IRAS requirements.

The decision to register voluntarily can have wider accounting, compliance and cash flow implications.

Businesses should understand these implications before applying for voluntary registration.

## Taxable Turnover

Taxable turnover is an important factor when determining whether GST registration requirements may apply.

Not every amount received by a business necessarily forms part of taxable turnover.

The treatment can depend on the nature of the supplies and the applicable GST rules.

Businesses should therefore avoid assuming that total revenue automatically equals taxable turnover.

## Current and Future Turnover

GST registration requirements can involve consideration of both past and expected future taxable turnover depending on the circumstances.

A business that expects its taxable turnover to increase significantly should monitor its position rather than waiting until the business has already exceeded an applicable threshold.

Regular monitoring can help businesses identify potential registration obligations early.

## What Happens After GST Registration?

A GST-registered business generally needs to comply with ongoing GST obligations.

Depending on its circumstances, this may include:

- Charging GST on taxable supplies
- Issuing tax invoices where required
- Maintaining appropriate accounting records
- Claiming eligible input tax
- Filing GST returns
- Paying GST due to IRAS
- Keeping supporting documentation
- Following applicable GST record-keeping requirements

GST compliance continues after registration, so businesses should have suitable accounting and reporting processes in place.

## Important Considerations

GST registration can depend on factors including:

- Taxable turnover
- Nature of supplies
- Zero-rated supplies
- Exempt supplies
- Business activities
- Current turnover
- Expected future turnover
- Registration circumstances
- Applicable IRAS rules

For this reason, businesses with similar revenue figures may not necessarily have identical GST obligations.
